# Why I'm Exploring Healthcare Technology as a Software Engineer

As a Software Engineering Technology student at Universitas Gadjah Mada with background in full-stack web applications, I have often wondered where engineering can create the most tangible, high-stakes impact.

General SaaS applications and e-commerce platforms are exciting, but healthcare presents a fundamentally different challenge: **fragmentation and data interoperability**.

## The Problem of Data Silos in Healthcare

When you visit a clinic, your health records often stay locked inside that single hospital or clinic's database (SIMRS / RME). If you move to another hospital, doctors often have to start over diagnosing your medical history or asking you to repeat laboratory tests.

In Indonesia, the Ministry of Health has introduced **SATUSEHAT**, built on the international **HL7 FHIR (Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources)** standard, aiming to connect thousands of health facilities into a standardized data exchange pipeline.

## My Engineering Approach

Rather than approaching this purely from policy or theory, I approach it as a backend systems engineer:
- How do we map messy relational database schemas into FHIR resources like `Patient`, `Encounter`, and `Observation`?
- How do we handle network retries and data consistency when syncing with government sandbox APIs?
- How do we ensure strict privacy and audit logging?

This journal documents my technical explorations, lessons, and experiments as I delve deeper into healthcare engineering.
